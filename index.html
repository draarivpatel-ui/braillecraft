        How BrailleCraft works
        
    
  
        
  
        
    
        
      
        Who it is for
        
      
        Students, teachers, makers and volunteers who make tactile signs and learning materials with and for blind and low-vision neighbors. The Community kit turns a hallway of signs into one print job, a handoff sheet that names who designs, prints, checks and mounts, and study cards so sighted classmates learn the braille they are putting on the wall. Have a braille reader check every sign by touch before it goes up.
        
    
        
    
        
      
        The braille cell
        
      
        Every cell has six dot positions in two columns. Dots 1, 2 and 3 run down the left column and dots 4, 5 and 6 down the right.
        
      
        
    
        
    
        
      
        Grade 1 and Grade 2
        
      
        Grade 1 spells every letter. Grade 2 (contracted) Unified English Braille swaps common words and letter groups for shorter signs, so signs and books take less space. BrailleCraft finds the longest valid match at each position and records which rule produced every cell, which is what the inspector shows when you hover a dot.

        Indicators
        
      
        Some cells change how the next cells read instead of standing for a letter.
        
      
        
    
        
    
        
      
        Tactile sign specs
        
      
        
    
        
    
        
      
        Perkins chords
        
      
        F, D and S press dots 1, 2 and 3. J, K and L press dots 4, 5 and 6. Hold every key of a cell at once and release together to emboss it, like a real brailler. Space embosses a blank cell, and touch screens can hold the keycaps.
        
    
        
    
        
      
        How it was built
        
      
        Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript modules with no dependencies, no build step and no network calls. A strict Content Security Policy blocks every outside request. The Self-Tests tab runs the same invariants as the Node test suite inside this page, from UEB vectors and ADA pitch bands to STL watertightness and chord mapping.
        
      
        AI coding assistants (Devin SWE-2 and Claude) helped write the code, tests and docs under Aariv Patel's direction. Every braille rule and dimension shown here is checked by that test suite.
